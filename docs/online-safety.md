# Online safety: the Online Safety Act assessments and the controls behind them

Read this when you add anything that lets one person's words reach another (comments, messages, sharing, public pages, profiles, search of people), change who can contact whom, or are asked whether Wordwright is open to under-18s. Written 26 October 2026 for #408. It is our working understanding of the Online Safety Act 2023 (OSA) and Ofcom's guidance, built from law-firm summaries and Ofcom's published pages, **not legal advice: the owner should have a lawyer check it before the public launch.** The data side (UK GDPR, the ICO Children's Code) is in [dpia.md](dpia.md); the facts about what we hold are in [data-map.md](data-map.md).

## The position in one paragraph

Wordwright is open to writers of any age. We do **not** state an age limit at sign-up, because a tick-box or a statement is not "highly effective age assurance" in Ofcom's sense and so cannot support a claim that children cannot reach the service (see [the decision](adr/accounts-and-security/open-to-under-18s-no-age-gate.md)). Instead we assume children may use it and keep the risk low by design: nothing is public, nobody can find or message a stranger, and the only words a person ever sees from someone else come from people they deliberately chose to work with. We keep that true by recording the controls here and treating a change to them as a reason to redo these assessments.

## Is Wordwright in scope?

Probably yes, in a small way. Part 3 of the OSA covers a **user-to-user service**: one where content generated by a user can be encountered by another user. Comments on a draft, brainstorm threads in a shared publication, notes, and review comments are content one person writes and another reads, so we assume the duties apply to us. We have a link to the UK (UK users, UK owner). The exemptions in Schedule 1 are drafted narrowly. The "limited functionality" one covers services where users can only comment on the provider's own content, which is not our case. Treat the service as in scope.

What that means in practice is three duties, each answered below: an **illegal-content risk assessment**, a **children's access assessment** (and, if children can access, a **children's risk assessment**), and the safety duties that follow: reporting and complaints, and terms that say what is not allowed.

## Illegal-content risk assessment

Assessed 26 October 2026. Ofcom's method is to list the places a service lets harm happen and score likelihood and impact. For a service this size the useful question is which of its **risk factors** are present.

| Risk factor Ofcom looks at                             | Wordwright                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strangers can find or contact each other               | **No.** There is no directory, profile page, search of people or direct message. You can only invite someone whose email address you already know.                              |
| Content is public or can be shared widely              | **No.** Nothing is public. Publication pictures are served to members only. Drafts and comments are visible only inside a publication or to the invited reviewer.               |
| Livestreaming, anonymous posting, or re-sharing        | **No.** Every comment carries its author's name. There is no repost, forward or public link.                                                                                    |
| Fast growth of a network, "friend-of-friend" discovery | **No.** Accounts are approved one by one by the owner during the private alpha (#328).                                                                                          |
| Images or video that people upload                     | **Almost none.** Publication pictures only (PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF, up to 1 MB), shown to members.                                                                              |
| Users can contact children, or children can be found   | **No** discovery of anyone, of any age.                                                                                                                                         |
| Generated content (AI)                                 | The AI's output goes only to the writer who asked for it, and is a suggestion that is not published ([responsible-ai.md](responsible-ai.md)). A comment is never sent to an AI. |

**Conclusion: low risk** for each kind of priority illegal content (terrorism, child sexual exploitation and abuse, grooming, harassment and stalking, hate, fraud, and the rest of the Act's list). The channels these harms need (reaching strangers, public audiences, anonymity, discovery) are absent. The residual routes are:

1. **Unwanted invitations or review requests**, which can carry a short message to a person who did not ask (harassment, or a lure). Measures are in [Stranger-contact controls](#stranger-contact-controls-and-why-we-have-them).
2. **Abuse inside a publication** between people who chose to work together. This is a falling-out between collaborators more than a platform risk, but it is the case a "report" button exists for.
3. **Illegal material in a draft or a comment that only a collaborator sees.** We do not scan content and should not (see [privacy](data-map.md)); we act on a report.

Review this assessment **once a year, before the public launch, and whenever a new way for one person's words to reach another is proposed** (a public page, a profile, user search, direct messages, any "share" that is not an emailed invitation to a named person).

## Children's access assessment

Assessed 26 October 2026. The question the Act asks is whether it is _possible_ for children to access the service and, if so, whether the service is _likely to be accessed by_ a significant number of them.

- It is **possible**: we do not use highly effective age assurance, so we cannot conclude that children cannot access it. An 18+ statement would not change that.
- It is **likely** that some under-18s will use it: writing is something young people do, and the product is aimed at writers generally. We therefore treat Wordwright as a service children can use.

Having concluded that children can access it, we carry out a children's risk assessment, summarised here.

### Children's risk assessment

Ofcom groups harmful content into **primary priority** content (pornography; content encouraging, promoting or giving instructions for suicide, self-harm or eating disorders), **priority** content (bullying, abuse and hate, violence, dangerous stunts and challenges, harmful substances) and other content that is harmful to children.

- **Primary priority content:** the duty to use highly effective age assurance applies to services that allow this content. Wordwright has no mechanism by which someone can put such content in front of a stranger: it is only visible to the person who wrote it and collaborators they invited. We do not allow it in our terms (see below), we do not host or promote it, and we are not a service on which it circulates. So we do not use age assurance. If a report shows otherwise, we reconsider.
- **Priority content, bullying in particular:** the real risk is an invited collaborator or reviewer being unkind. Measures: a person can only be contacted if someone typed their address; any invitation can be declined, and the invitee can **block that sender or everyone**; blocks are silent for the sender; a comment is hidden by the editor, never shown to others; the person can leave a publication or delete their account at any time.
- **Contact with adults:** a child cannot be found, searched for or messaged by a stranger. The only way in is for the child (or their parent, for an under-18) to accept an emailed invitation.
- **Design that keeps children engaged for its own sake:** none. No streaks, feeds, notifications that pull people back, or recommendation. Wordwright's aim is finished, published writing ([responsible-ai.md](responsible-ai.md)).
- **AI features:** need the writer's own API key, and AI providers require account holders to be adults, so a child using the AI features means an adult supplied the key. The AI only ever responds to what the writer sends, and the distress and flattery rules in [responsible-ai.md](responsible-ai.md) apply to everyone.

**Conclusion: low risk to children.** The remaining step on this list is a way to report abuse (below).

## Stranger-contact controls, and why we have them

These are the controls that hold the risk assessment up. They exist for several reasons, and the online-safety one is now explicit. Each is described in [accounts.md](accounts.md).

| Control                                                                                                  | Why                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sharing is by emailed invitation to a named address** (no search of people, no public publication)     | A stranger cannot be found or reached. The only content a person sees from others comes from someone they chose to work with.            |
| **Only an account with a confirmed email can invite**                                                    | The sender is a real, reachable person; sharing is gated, writing is not.                                                                |
| **Caps: 5 invitations a day to one address, 20 a day per sender**                                        | Bounds spam and harassment by invitation.                                                                                                |
| **Decline and block on every invitation**, silent for the sender, keyed by address                       | The recipient can refuse a sender, or everyone, and the sender learns nothing they could retaliate on.                                   |
| **Reviewers get reviewer-only accounts** that reach only their own reviews, with every other route a 404 | A reviewer sees one frozen draft and their own comments, not the idea, notes or other people. A test walks every route as a reviewer.    |
| **Comments are visible only to the editor and to their author**; only the editor resolves them           | Comments are not a conversation among strangers; other readers never see them.                                                           |
| **Owner approval of every new account during the alpha (#328)**                                          | We know who is on the service. This is the strongest control and should stay until the launch decision is made on the assessments above. |
| **No public pages, profiles, directory or direct messages**                                              | These are the features that turn a writing tool into a place where strangers meet. Adding one means redoing this assessment.             |
| **Nothing is scanned or sent to an AI by us** (comments are never sent)                                  | Privacy of drafts; and we do not take on a monitoring role we do not need.                                                               |

Do **not** add any of the following without redoing the assessments here and the DPIA: public sharing of a draft or publication; user profiles; any search or suggestion of people; direct messages; commenting on something other than a draft you were invited to; an open sign-up with no approval.
