# Data protection impact assessment

Read this when you change how AI features send text, how reviewers or invitations work, what happens to a person's name when they leave, or you are asked why we decided a DPIA was needed. The facts it relies on are in [data-map.md](data-map.md). Written 26 October 2026 for #320; not legal advice, and the owner may want a lawyer to check it. Review it before launch, and again whenever one of the four areas below changes.

## Why one is needed

The ICO lists new technologies and the use of AI among the things that call for a DPIA, and Wordwright sends people's unpublished writing to a third-party AI. We have no special-category data as a purpose and no large-scale monitoring, but a writer's drafts can hold anything, so we assess rather than argue the threshold.

## The processing

Writers keep ideas, notes and drafts in publications they share with others. When a writer chooses, selected text goes to their own AI provider using their own key, and the answer comes back as suggestions. A writer can ask someone outside the publication to read a draft and comment (a reviewer), and can invite someone to join a publication. A person who leaves can keep or remove their name on work that stays behind. The purpose is to help people think about and finish their own writing ([responsible-ai.md](responsible-ai.md)). Volume is small (a private alpha), and the data is described in [data-map.md](data-map.md).

## Necessity and proportionality

- **We collect little:** name, email, username, a password hash or a Google identifier. No date of birth, location, phone number, IP address or device fingerprint is stored.
- **AI use is the writer's act each time**: no background calls, a button per call, nothing sent from the draft except what the feature needs (the table in the data map), comments and reviewers' words never sent.
- **No server-wide key and no training:** we hold no AI contract. The writer's provider terms apply, and the app tells them to use an API key (not a chat subscription), to check retention, and not to enter what they could not send a trusted contractor.
- **Nothing is published or sent onward by us**: AI output stays a suggestion the writer accepts or ignores.

## Children

Added 26 October 2026 for #408. We are open to under-18s, so the ICO's [Children's Code](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/) (the Age Appropriate Design Code) applies to us as a service likely to be used by children. We use it as the design reference, in the order of its standards:

- **Best interests, and age-appropriate application:** the product exists to help people finish their own writing; it has no feed, streak, notification that pulls people back, or recommendation. Because we cannot tell ages, we apply the same low-risk defaults to everyone rather than a weaker set to adults.
- **Transparency:** the privacy notice and terms (#321) are written in plain language; the data map's statements about what we collect are short enough to say on the page. The notice beside the AI key field says what leaves the server.
- **Detrimental use of data, data minimisation, profiling, nudge techniques:** we collect only name, email, username and a password hash or Google identifier; we do no profiling, no advertising, no analytics beyond what runs the service, and no nudging to share or to use the AI more.
- **Default settings, data sharing, geolocation:** nothing is public by default and there is nothing to switch on; we share nothing with third parties except processors and the writer's own AI provider on their button press; we do not collect location.
- **Parental controls and online tools:** none are offered. A child can delete their account and download their writing; blocking and declining are available for invitations. A way to report is planned (#412).
- **Legal basis:** we rely on contract, not consent. Our terms must be clear that a person under 18 may use Wordwright for their own benefit; a lawyer should check this. If we ever rely on consent, UK GDPR Article 8 sets 13 as the age a child may consent to an online service.

Residual risk: **low**. This does not make a DPIA for children a separate exercise; revisit it when a feature that lets words reach other people is proposed.

## Outcome

None of the risks is high after the measures in place, so we do **not** need to consult the ICO before processing starts. This is conditional on closing the gaps that touch these risks before outside users arrive: the privacy notice and terms (#321) with the AI provider and role wording (gaps 2 and 6 in the data map), the children wording (gap 5: no age statement, plain language for young people), and the deletion-after-restore step (8).
